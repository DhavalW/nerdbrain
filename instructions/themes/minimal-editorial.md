# Minimal editorial

## Position

Quiet confidence for readers: the content is the interface, and the design's job is to
get out of its way beautifully. For products where people read, write, or think.

## The one idea

**Typography carries everything.** No cards, few borders, almost no chrome — hierarchy,
rhythm, and generous measure do the structural work that boxes do elsewhere.

## Systems

- **Type:** a serif with real character for headings and long-form (e.g. a text serif à
  la Source Serif / Literata), a plain sans for UI labels. Scale ratio ~1.4 — sizes must
  *jump*: 15/16 body, 24, 34, 48. Line height 1.6–1.7 on body; measure hard-capped at
  68ch.
- **Space:** 8px base; vertical rhythm dominates — space above a heading ~3× the space
  below it, so sections group unmistakably.
- **Color:** near-monochrome. Ink-on-paper neutrals warmed slightly (never pure #000/#fff);
  one restrained accent for links and actions only. Dark mode is warm dark grey, ink
  softened, same accent dimmed.
- **Surfaces:** radius 0–4px; hairline borders only where whitespace can't separate;
  shadows barely exist (one soft level for overlays).
- **Motion:** almost none. 150ms opacity/position fades; nothing bounces, nothing slides
  in from off-screen. Reading is the animation.

## Feels like / never

- Feels like: a well-set book, a good newspaper, an instrument that respects you.
- Never: cards-in-grids, gradient anything, icon noise, competing accents, decoration
  that doesn't inform. Density of *chrome* is the enemy — density of *text* is fine.

## Fits

Writing tools, blogs, docs, reading apps, newsletters, portfolios with words, anything
consumed slowly. Wrong voice for dashboards, playful consumer apps, data-dense admin.
